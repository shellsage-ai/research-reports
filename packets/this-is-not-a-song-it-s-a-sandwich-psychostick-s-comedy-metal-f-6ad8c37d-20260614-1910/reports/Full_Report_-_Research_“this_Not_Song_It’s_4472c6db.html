Psychostick’s Sandwich That Refuses to Be a Song

Executive Summary

This Is Not a Song, It’s a Sandwich is a 2009 Psychostick track from the album Sandwich, and the official lyric-video framing presents it exactly as the title advertises: a metal song that spends its runtime denying that it is a song at all [1][2]. The strongest evidence comes from Psychostick’s own lyrics page, official music page, store listing, Bandcamp page, and streaming listings, which jointly confirm the song title, album placement, 2009 release context, and the band’s self-described comedy-metal identity [2][3][4][5][6][7]. The song is credited on Psychostick’s official lyrics page with music by Josh Key and lyrics by Key and Jimmy Grant, and the official album materials place it inside a food-heavy record that also includes Don’t Eat My Food, The Hunger Within, Grocery Escape Plan, Too Many Food, Do You Want a Taco?, and We Ran Out of CD Space [2][3][4][5].

The song’s joke is not complicated, but it is engineered with real craft: it creates an obvious contradiction, repeats it with total conviction, and then escalates from plausible sandwich words into nonsense inventory, making the listener hold two frames at once, namely this is clearly a song and the singer is aggressively litigating its sandwich status [2][15][16]. That makes it a clean example of musical incongruity, because the track behaves like metal while its lyric insists on a literal object-category mistake [2][15][16]. It also uses benign violation: the band violates expectations of metal seriousness, lyrical profundity, and album-format coherence, but the violation is low-stakes, silly, and communal [9][10][15].

Psychostick’s broader style explains why the bit lands instead of collapsing into random yelling [9][10]. The band’s own biography describes them as comedic metal and self-proclaimed humorcore, and an interview-era profile emphasized that the group treats musicianship, instrumentation, and comedy as things to execute seriously [9][10]. The result is intentionally stupid-smart music: the premise is dumb on purpose, while the timing, riffs, repetition, category errors, and audience-addressed argument are disciplined enough to make the dumbness reproducible [2][9][10][16].

The main caveat is that the official sources are rich on release facts and lyrics but thin on direct band commentary about this exact song’s composition, so interpretation of the joke mechanics relies on close reading plus broader humor and musical-humor scholarship [2][15][16]. The evidence is strong that the song belongs to Sandwich, that Psychostick intended the album as a food-comedy metal object, and that fans receive the band through repeatable jokes, quotes, and shareable absurdity [3][4][5][10][17][19].
